2.Customer brief

"PayGate retries failed webhooks three times; we want one investigation per incident, not three." — Harbourline payments lead, Thursday stand-up

Last week the payments lead wired PayGate's delivery-failure notifications straight into a Devin Automation with a Webhook trigger. Every notification started a session. One bad morning produced eleven sessions for what turned out to be two problems: a Lumen Home order whose confirmation webhook kept hitting a 503, and a Lumen Home payment.failed event rejected for a bad signature. The rest were retries of the same event, a delivery that recovered on its second attempt, a notification PayGate sent twice, a test.ping and one notification with no event id at all.

The lead wants the Automation kept, because the two real failures were caught quickly. What has to change is what reaches Devin: one investigation per real failure, a second one only when the same event fails again after a manual replay, and nothing for noise. They will ask you how you know nothing real is being dropped.

The file labs/07-automation-preflight/fixtures/paygate-notifications.json holds that morning's eleven notifications in arrival order, plus the next-day replay. Monitor 48213802 (webhook failure rate above 5% over 15 minutes) and runbook RB-201 are the context the investigations themselves would use.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Payments lead (Lumen Home and Trailhead Supply) — owns PayGate and the Automation. Wants fewer sessions, but a missed real failure is worse than an extra session. Reads your update in three minutes.
  • Finance controller — pays for every session the Automation starts. Will ask what the filtering costs and what it saves.
  • Platform on-call — reads the investigations. Wants each one to name the event, the order, the error class and the attempt.

Constraints:

  • One Automation, one Webhook trigger. No scheduled Automations and no weekly drills; any Automation you create live is disabled or deleted before you finish.
  • The filter must survive PayGate sending the same notification twice and the same event failing again on a later day.
  • The duplicate-webhook defect in the fulfilment worker is a planted course defect. Do not fix it on main.
  • Money is a decimal string with a currency code; never a float, never mixed currencies in one total.
  • No real PayGate account and no customer data: the fixture notifications are all you get.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not on the call. Every item can be met on the fixture path with zero credentials.

  1. A Preflight script exists. labs/07-automation-preflight/preflight.mjs (or .py) reads the event, reads and writes its state file, and writes one decision. It is not committed to main; keep it on your own branch or attach it to your report.
  2. The numbers. node labs/07-automation-preflight/tools/preflight-replay.mjs --script labs/07-automation-preflight/preflight.mjs --state-dir labs/07-automation-preflight/.state prints a RESULT: line, and labs/07-automation-preflight/PREFLIGHT-REPORT.md quotes it with the count in, the count out, and which events became investigations.
  3. Every skip has a reason. Each skipped line in the replay carries a reason a payments lead could read (transient attempt, recovered, already seen, not a delivery, malformed). No line says only "skipped".
  4. Idempotent. Running the same replay a second time against the same --state-dir opens zero new investigations. Paste both RESULT: lines.
  5. The replay injector. After the duplicate-event-new-version injector, the report says whether the next-day failure is a new investigation, and the replay output agrees with what you said.
  6. Filter choice justified. One paragraph compares the natural-language filter with a script for this customer, names what each one can and cannot see, and says which you chose.
  7. Failure behaviour. The report says what happens to an event when the Preflight times out, exits non-zero or writes an invalid decision, what timeout you set and why, and shows the timeout and invalid-output injector lines.
  8. Run now. One sentence on what the Run now button does to the filter and how the payments lead should test the Preflight instead.
  9. Load. One sentence on whether this Automation needs queueing (concurrent runs, queue depth) on a bad morning, with the number you would set.
  10. Credential honesty. A short "not exercised" list: whether a live Automation received anything, and that PayGate was a fixture. If you created a live Automation, a screenshot showing it disabled or deleted.
  11. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) or against a timer, and the gap report (or your self-score) saved as labs/07-automation-preflight/PREFLIGHT-REPORT.coach.md. Do not commit it.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This lab uses the four-part articulation drill with two think-aloud checkpoints. The coach plays the Harbourline payments lead: practical, worried about missing a real failure, impatient with tooling words that do not come with a number.

60-second framing

Say what the customer gets before you say how. Stems you may use:

  • "Today, … notifications turn into … sessions. After this change, …"
  • "The problem is not the Automation, it is what reaches it: …"
  • "I am using Devin's … because…"
  • "You will know it works when…"

Sinhala-first note: lead with the outcome sentence. Do not walk through the morning's notifications one by one before saying what changes.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+15)

The coach asks what you are building first and why. Use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. Name the one fact that decided your design. If you need a second to look at the fixture, say "Give me ten seconds to read this".

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+75)

You are writing the skip logic. Say what makes two notifications "the same thing" in your script, and what makes them different. One rule per sentence. End on a full stop, not on "…right?".

Update to the payments lead (3 minutes)

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Put the before/after numbers in the first sentence. Give each failure mode its own sentence. Close with what you need the lead to decide. Drop fillers ("actually", "basically", "so, yeah").

Objection round

The coach raises one technical and one commercial objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
