2.Customer brief

From: Oksana Brandt, CISO, Harbourline To: Platform engineering Subject: Devin can stay — if it works inside our VPC

Legal signed off on Devin last quarter on one condition I am now enforcing: the PayGate sandbox keys and our repositories do not leave Harbourline infrastructure. Today every Devin session clones the repo onto a Devin-hosted machine, and engineers paste sandbox keys into session secrets. That stops on Friday.

I am told Devin has something called Outposts. Show me, by Friday, a worker image we build and own, running in our VPC, that our engineers reach the same way they reach Devin today — web, Slack, and whatever the default is. Two of them, because the payments team and the storefront team will both be on it at 09:00. Tell me what happens when they are both busy, what happens when one dies overnight, and who keeps that image patched.

If you cannot get an Outpost created, I still want the image, the runbook and the evidence. Tell me plainly which parts you saw run and which parts you could only rehearse.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Oksana Brandt (CISO) — the decision maker. Precise about data paths, unimpressed by marketing words; she will challenge you on three points.
  • Platform engineering (you) — owns outpost/ and the hosts the workers run on.
  • Payments and storefront teams — the two daily users; they start sessions from Slack and from the web app and should not have to learn anything new.
  • Constraints
    • No credential, worker token or PayGate key is ever baked into an image, committed, or pasted into a chat.
    • Workers get outbound HTTPS only; no inbound ports.
    • The repo is baked into the image so a session starts without a network clone.
    • Workers are long-lived, so sessions get no root.
    • Never ask anyone for credentials to finish the lab; if you lack Outpost permission, take the observer path in the credential matrix.
    • Coaching is started by you, manually, when you are ready.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not in the room. Each item has a fixture-path version that needs no credentials.

  1. Worker image. outpost/build.sh builds the image from the committed HEAD, or its Ubuntu smoke variant (--build-arg BASE_IMAGE=…) builds when the official registry is unreachable, and node labs/14-outposts/tools/outpost-sim.mjs check-dockerfile outpost/Dockerfile prints nine ok lines.
  2. Repo and dependencies inside. outpost/smoke.sh run in the built image exits 0: git, ffmpeg, Node 22, Chrome at DEVIN_CHROME_PATH, the repo under /workspace/repos/learn-devin-new-features with dependencies installed, non-root, no passwordless sudo, no token in the environment.
  3. Runbook. outpost/RUNBOOK.md lets a new engineer create the Outpost, store the token, start two workers, route sessions from web, Slack and the default platform, recover a dead worker and patch the image — without asking you.
  4. Queue evidence. labs/14-outposts/OUTPOST-REPORT.md contains the replay of fixtures/two-workers.json (or the live equivalent) and states, with times, which session waited and why.
  5. Failure evidence. The same report shows the claim race, the duplicated acceptor id and the night-time worker loss, each with the one-line lesson.
  6. Injector reviews. The report answers the no-Chrome image and the baked-token pull request with a verdict and the failing check names.
  7. Live versus rehearsed. The report ends with a table of every step and whether it ran live, ran on the fixture path, or was not exercised and why.
  8. Coach run. You ran the coach for this lab and the report is on your coaching branch.

With fixtures only

You label every live step as not exercised and say exactly what you would run.

Cannot be exercised without live credentials

  • Registering a real worker with `devin worker start --outpost … --token …` and running a session on it (needs an Outpost and its one-time token).
  • Starting a session from Slack with `!outpost` and setting the organization default platform (need the Slack app and org-admin rights).
  • Watching the live wait banner while two real containers are busy (the queue model reproduces the rules, not the UI).

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

Start the coach yourself when you are ready (@skills:coach-14-outposts <your-handle>). It plays the CISO from the brief, times every segment and grades with the shared rubric.

60-second framing for the CISO

One minute: what Outposts changes about where Harbourline's code and keys live, what stays the same, and what you will show her by Friday. Lead with the boundary, not the product name.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+15)

Forty-five seconds: where you are in the setup, what you just verified, and the next thing you will check. Name any secret you touched and where it is now.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+60)

Forty-five seconds after the queue and failure replays: what the replay proved, what it cannot prove, and what you check next.

Three-minute update to the CISO

Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Cover the image, how engineers reach it, capacity, and which parts you saw live.

Objection round

Three objections, ninety seconds each, in character. Acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer.
