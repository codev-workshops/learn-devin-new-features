2.Customer brief

Hi — I run the platform team at Harbourline. We operate two online brands on one codebase and one PostgreSQL database: Lumen Home (USD, lighting and home goods, tax added at checkout) and Trailhead Supply (EUR, outdoor gear, VAT-inclusive prices, free shipping over €80). Together they process roughly 400 orders a week in the quiet season and several times that during Lumen's "Lamp Week" flash sale.

We have just been given a Devin seat and I have a list of things that hurt: a new engineer takes a week to run the store locally and someone reset the shared database on day two; a merchandiser once saw another brand's order; the Trailhead reconciliation report is off by a few cents on about 3% of orders; and during the last flash sale we sold lamps we did not have. I do not expect you to fix any of that today.

What I want from you in the next twenty minutes is a one-page plan: which problem you would put in front of Devin first, which Devin capability you would use for it, why that one and not the others, and how you — and I — would know it worked. I will read the plan, then ask you to explain it to me out loud as if I were paying for it.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • In the room: the platform lead (me), one merchandiser from each brand, the on-call engineer who was paged during Lamp Week.
  • Forbidden this week: no schema changes, no changes on main, nothing that touches the production database. Demos happen on throwaway branches.
  • Decision deadline: the Lamp Week flash sale starts in four weeks; whatever you pick first must show evidence before then.
  • Budget: one Devin seat; assume a modest ACU allowance, so the first task must be small enough to finish in one session.
  • Output: a Markdown file, labs/00-sample/PLAN.md, no longer than one printed page. Do not commit it.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not in the room.

  1. A plan exists at labs/00-sample/PLAN.md, one printed page or less, with four headed parts in this order: the problem, the approach, the evidence, the next step.
  2. One problem, one capability. The plan names exactly one of the four problems in the brief as the first task and exactly one Devin capability for it. A reader can tell in one sentence why that capability and not an alternative.
  3. Business impact is quantified. At least one sentence states the cost of the chosen problem in orders, money or engineer-days using numbers from the brief (or an explicit assumption).
  4. Evidence is observable, not a command. The "evidence" part lists what artefact, number, screenshot or recording would prove success. Lines such as "run the tests" do not count; "the oversell script leaves on-hand at 0, not −4" does.
  5. Constraints are respected explicitly. The plan states at least one thing it will not do because of the stakeholder constraints (schema freeze, no changes on main, budget).
  6. The injector is absorbed. After the T+10 "deadline moved" announcement the plan has a dated one-line amendment saying what changed and what did not.
  7. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8), or against a timer if you work offline, and the gap report (or your self-score) is saved next to the plan as labs/00-sample/PLAN.report.md. Do not commit either file.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This warm-up uses a shortened version of the four-part articulation drill. The coach plays the Harbourline platform lead.

60-second framing

Open with the headline, then one reason, then the proof. Stems you may use:

  • "The customer's problem is… It costs them…"
  • "I will use… because…"
  • "I will know it worked when…"

Sinhala-first note: resist front-loading the context ("because A, because B, therefore X"). Say X first.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+10)

When the coach asks "What are you looking at right now, and what will you check next?", answer with the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. If you need to read, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent. Finish on a full stop, not on "…no?".

Two-minute customer update

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. One number per sentence. Breathe after each heading word; the pause sounds like confidence.

Objection round

The coach raises one technical and one commercial objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections themselves are not printed here — that is the point.

Start the Devin voice coach

In the Devin web app, select this repo in Agent mode, type exactly the line below, then click Start voice call:

@skills:coach-00-sample <your-handle>

Replace with your handle. If the call connects before the skill activates, press Enter first and then start the call in the same session.

Mute hint: between checkpoints you may mute and keep working; the call stays connected. Hold Space to talk if your client offers push-to-talk, and say “done” when you finish a timed segment.

Caveat: clarity and pronunciation feedback from the coach is transcript-based, approximate. The coach works from the speech transcript in the session history, not from audio, so it cannot judge intonation, stress or vowel quality. Record yourself and listen back for real pronunciation work.
