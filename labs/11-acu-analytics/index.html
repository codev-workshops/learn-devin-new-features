2.Customer brief

"Finance wants a cap per team. Trailhead says they'll be blocked in the middle of the Winter Drop." — Harbourline finance controller, Friday budget review

Harbourline runs three Devin organizations under one enterprise: Harbourline Platform, Lumen Home and Trailhead Supply. The contract renews its ACU window every month on the 23rd. Last month the enterprise overspent its plan by a little under ten percent, and finance wants a hard number per brand team before the next window opens. Nobody has been blocked yet, and everyone wants to keep it that way.

This morning (Saturday 14 November 2026) two things happened. Trailhead's engineering lead could not start a Devin session even though her own usage is well under what she was told she has. And a Lumen engineer who is on the on-call rota this month was stopped mid-investigation with a message about his personal limit, while Lumen as a whole has used about two thirds of its budget. Trailhead's Winter Drop sale runs 18–20 November and needs Devin for flash-sale releases.

To make it worse, the finance controller and the Trailhead lead brought two different dashboards to the meeting and their totals do not match. Finance wants to know which number is "the real one" before signing off on any cap.

labs/11-acu-analytics/fixtures/harbourline-consumption.json is a fictional copy of that enterprise: organizations and their limits, usage tiers, IdP group mappings, members, and usage split by surface. labs/11-acu-analytics/tools/acu-sim.mjs applies the documented limit rules to it (run it with --help).

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Finance controller — wants a cap per brand team and one number per sentence. Reads a three-minute update and forwards it.
  • Trailhead Supply engineering lead — blocked this morning; the sale starts on Wednesday. Will not accept "wait for the reset".
  • Lumen Home on-call engineer — blocked mid-investigation; expects his on-call role to give him more room.
  • Enterprise admin (Platform) — the only person who can change organization limits, tiers, mappings and overrides. Will ask what exactly to change and until when.

Constraints:

  • No change to a real enterprise during the lab. Every limit change is simulated with acu-sim.mjs flags; screenshots of your own tenant are read-only and stay in the git-ignored figures/tenant/ folder.
  • Every number you quote must come from a named view or a simulator line, and you must say whether it includes local (Desktop, CLI) usage and Devin Review.
  • No Automations, no scheduled reports: finance gets one update from you, today.
  • No real customer or employee data. The members in the fixture are fictional.

What “clean” looks like

The simulator is stateless: every run starts from fixtures/harbourline-consumption.json and only the flags you pass change it, so lab:reset (which reseeds the shop database) is optional here. Clean means no ACU-DECISION.md of your own and no figures/tenant/ folder. --views prints an enterprise total of 8870 ACU for the current billing window (23 Oct → 14 Nov 2026) and three organization rows.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not on the call. Every item can be met on the fixture path with zero credentials.

  1. A decision file exists. labs/11-acu-analytics/ACU-DECISION.md (git-ignored; keep it on your own branch or attach it to your report) holds both decisions, the numbers ledger and the finance update.
  2. Decision A (Trailhead). The file names which limit blocked the Trailhead lead this morning, quotes the simulator RESULT: line, and shows one change that does not unblock her and one that does, each with its RESULT: line.
  3. The sale holds. After the sale-spike injector, the file gives the number you would set for Trailhead, who sets it, until when it applies, and the RESULT: line for 20 November.
  4. Decision B (Lumen on-call). The file explains why the on-call engineer has the limit he has (which rule picked it), compares at least two fixes (for example a temporary override versus changing his tier), picks one, and quotes the RESULT: line.
  5. Reset day. After the reset-day injector, one sentence says what happened to the override and to both meters on 23 November.
  6. Review storm. After the review-storm injector, one sentence says which meter moved and which did not, with both numbers.
  7. Numbers ledger. A table with one row per number you quote: value, source view (or simulator line), date range, and two yes/no columns — includes local usage? and includes Devin Review? At least five rows, including the enterprise total, one organization's total, one Cloud-tab number and one per-user number.
  8. The two dashboards. A short paragraph that explains, with numbers from the ledger, why the finance controller's total and the Trailhead lead's total differ. The billing-org-drift and empty-cycle injectors each get one sentence.
  9. Newest vs Legacy. Two or three sentences on what the Version setting changes, what a "Cloud only" metric leaves out, and which figures you replaced with your own tenant's screenshots (or that all eight are still placeholders).
  10. "A cap per team". One paragraph answering finance's request directly: what mechanism gives a team cap, what mechanism gives a per-person cap, and what you recommend for Harbourline.
  11. Credential honesty. A short "not exercised" list: which admin actions were simulated, which analytics pages you looked at live (if any).
  12. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) or against a timer, and the gap report (or your self-score) saved as labs/11-acu-analytics/ACU-DECISION.coach.md. Do not commit it.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This lab uses the four-part articulation drill with two think-aloud checkpoints. The coach plays the Harbourline finance controller: polite, numerate, suspicious of any figure without a source, and impatient with product vocabulary.

60-second framing

Say what finance gets before you say how. Stems you may use:

  • "Two people were blocked this morning for two different reasons: …"
  • "There are two kinds of cap, and they do different jobs: …"
  • "I am using … because…"
  • "You will know it works when…"

Sinhala-first note: open with the outcome. Do not explain the contract, the three organizations and the tier list before you say what changes.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+10)

The coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next. Use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. Name the meter you read first and its number. If you need to read the simulator output, say "Give me ten seconds to read this".

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+40)

You are filling in the numbers ledger. Say, for two numbers on your screen, what each one counts and what it leaves out. One number per sentence. End on a full stop, not on "…right?".

Update to the finance controller (3 minutes)

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Finance asked for a short update: choose your numbers carefully, say where each one comes from, and do not read the ledger aloud. Close with the decision you need from finance.

Objection round

The coach raises one commercial and one technical objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
