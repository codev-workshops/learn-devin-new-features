This lab uses the four-part articulation drill with two think-aloud checkpoints, one per platform. The coach plays the Harbourline product owner: not an engineer, wants to see the same screen on two phones, and counts every hour of machine time.

### 60-second framing

Say what the customer gets before you say how. Stems you may use:

- "Customers will see … on their phone, on … and …"
- "The proof is …"
- "I am using Devin's … because…"
- "You will know it works when…"

Sinhala-first note: lead with the outcome sentence. Do not start with the toolchain.

### Think-aloud checkpoint (T+30, Android)

The coach asks where you are on Android. Use the four verbs: *I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…*. Name the one thing that decided your next step. If a build is still running, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" and read the last log line.

### Think-aloud checkpoint (T+120, iOS)

You have moved to the second platform. Say what is different there and what you carried over from Android. One platform per sentence; do not lose the thread between the two. End on a full stop, not on "…right?".

### Update to the product owner (3 minutes)

Skeleton: **Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask.** Show the evidence per platform. Give each risk its own sentence and say what was not tried. Close with the one decision you need. Drop fillers ("actually", "basically", "so, yeah").

### Objection round

The coach raises one commercial and one technical objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
