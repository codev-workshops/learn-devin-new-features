2.Customer brief

"Customers keep emailing to ask whether their order has left the warehouse. Put 'packed' and 'shipped' on their phone, and show me it works on both phones before we tell anyone." — Harbourline product owner, mobile roadmap review

Harbourline's support inbox gets roughly 140 "where is my order?" emails a week across Lumen Home and Trailhead Supply, and about two thirds of them are for orders that are already packed or shipped. The product owner wants the order-status screen in the Harbourline mobile app (apps/mobile, Expo SDK 54) to be the place customers look first: the status pill and the event timeline for packed and shipped orders, on Android and on iOS.

The product owner has been burned before: last quarter a "works on my machine" demo turned out to be an Android-only build, and iOS customers got a crash on launch. This time they want proof from both platforms — screenshots of the same order in the same state — and a plain list of what was not tried.

The app already has a cart that triggers a small "add to cart" haptic on iOS through an optional native module, apps/mobile/modules/harbourline-haptics. Android has no such module and must keep working. Treat the haptic as nice-to-have: the order-status proof matters more.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Product owner (mobile) — decides whether this ships. Wants one update with proof on both platforms, the risks and one clear ask. Not an engineer.
  • Mobile tech lead — cares that android/ and ios/ stay generated (Continuous Native Generation) and that the repo setup is reproducible for the next engineer, not just on your session.
  • Platform finance — pays for the machines. Will ask which parts needed a Mac and for how long.

Constraints:

  • Expo SDK 54, React Native 0.81, Expo Router. No SDK upgrade this week.
  • android/ and ios/ are generated by pnpm exec expo prebuild and are never committed or hand-edited.
  • The app runs in mock mode by default (EXPO_PUBLIC_API_MODE=mock): no backend, no credentials, no real customer data.
  • The planted defects listed in packages/contracts/seed-spec.yaml#planted_edge_cases are features of the course. Do not fix one on main.
  • iOS work is optional for the lab (it needs a macOS machine). If you cannot get one, the Android half plus an honest "not exercised" list is a complete answer.

What “clean” looks like

No apps/mobile/android/ or apps/mobile/ios/ folder exists, git status is clean, and pnpm --filter @harbourline/mobile run build ends with an Android expo export into apps/mobile/dist. No emulator is running yet (adb devices lists nothing). Without Docker, skip the first command: mock mode needs only Node.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not on the call. Items 1–5 and 9–11 can be met on Linux with zero credentials; items 6–8 need a macOS machine and are optional.

  1. Android build. A Release (or development) build of apps/mobile is installed on an Android emulator started with KVM acceleration. The emulator command line and adb devices output are in labs/13-expo-mobile/MOBILE-REPORT.md.
  2. Android order status. A screenshot of the order-detail screen for a packed or shipped order (status pill and timeline visible), taken with adb exec-out screencap -p.
  3. Android fallback. A screenshot of the Cart tab showing Haptics: JS fallback (no-op) after adding an item, so the native module's absence is visible and harmless.
  4. Generated native folders stay generated. git status shows no apps/mobile/android/ or apps/mobile/ios/ files, and the report says how you would detect and recover from prebuild drift.
  5. Gates. pnpm test, pnpm lint and pnpm build in the apps/mobile workspace pass; paste the summary lines.
  6. iOS build (optional). The same app built for the iOS Simulator on a macOS machine. The report names the Xcode version and how it was selected (DEVELOPER_DIR or xcode-select).
  7. iOS order status (optional). A Simulator screenshot of the same order in the same status as item 2.
  8. Native module (optional). A Simulator screenshot of the Cart tab showing Haptics: native (harbourline-haptics), and the line in ios/Podfile.lock that proves HarbourlineHaptics was autolinked.
  9. Reproducible setup. A blueprint (or a proposed one in the report) with one document per platform, each starting with its runs-on: line, containing only commands you actually ran. Say which document a future Android session and a future iOS session would boot from.
  10. Credential and platform honesty. A short "not exercised" list: at least physical-device haptics and store signing, plus anything else you skipped.
  11. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) or against a timer, and the gap report (or your self-score) saved as labs/13-expo-mobile/MOBILE-REPORT.coach.md. Do not commit it.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This lab uses the four-part articulation drill with two think-aloud checkpoints, one per platform. The coach plays the Harbourline product owner: not an engineer, wants to see the same screen on two phones, and counts every hour of machine time.

60-second framing

Say what the customer gets before you say how. Stems you may use:

  • "Customers will see … on their phone, on … and …"
  • "The proof is …"
  • "I am using Devin's … because…"
  • "You will know it works when…"

Sinhala-first note: lead with the outcome sentence. Do not start with the toolchain.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+30, Android)

The coach asks where you are on Android. Use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. Name the one thing that decided your next step. If a build is still running, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" and read the last log line.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+120, iOS)

You have moved to the second platform. Say what is different there and what you carried over from Android. One platform per sentence; do not lose the thread between the two. End on a full stop, not on "…right?".

Update to the product owner (3 minutes)

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Show the evidence per platform. Give each risk its own sentence and say what was not tried. Close with the one decision you need. Drop fillers ("actually", "basically", "so, yeah").

Objection round

The coach raises one commercial and one technical objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
