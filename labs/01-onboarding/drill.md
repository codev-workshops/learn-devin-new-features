This lab uses the standard four-part articulation drill. The coach plays Priya Nair, Harbourline's hiring manager: friendly, not technical, and responsible for Monday's new starter. Everything you say must survive being repeated by someone who has never opened a terminal.

### 60-second framing for a non-engineer

Say what the problem costs and what will be different on Monday before you say anything about tools. Stems you may use:

- "Today a new engineer spends … before they can … It costs us …"
- "The risk I care most about is …"
- "I am using Devin to … and I am deliberately not using it to …"
- "You will know it worked when …"

Sinhala-first note: lead with the outcome. Do not walk through the setup steps in order ("first Node, then npm, then Docker…") before saying what changes for the new starter.

### Think-aloud checkpoints (T+10 and T+40)

The coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next. Use the four verbs: *I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…*. At T+10 you are usually still auditing; at T+40 you are usually in the middle of an injector. Name the file you are reading. If you need a moment, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent.

### 3-minute update to the hiring manager

Skeleton: **Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask.** One number per sentence. For every change, say *where* it lives (machine setup, instructions Devin reads, reusable know-how) in plain words, then the proof. End with one concrete ask of Priya.

### Objection round

The coach raises two objections, 90 seconds each: one technical, one about people and adoption. Acknowledge, answer with evidence from your session, and close with a next step. Avoid "basically" and "actually"; do not end on "…right?".

Scores and timings are transcript-based, approximate.
