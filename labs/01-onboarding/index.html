2.Customer brief

I run engineering for Harbourline — the platform behind Lumen Home and Trailhead Supply. We hire two engineers a month, and every one of them loses their first week to getting the store running locally: the setup page points at a service we deleted months ago, the per-service notes disagree with each other, and nobody can say which instructions are current. That is roughly 40 engineer-days a year spent before anyone ships anything.

Worse: on day two of her first week, our last hire followed a "reset the database" line from an old note and wiped the shared development database that three brand teams were demoing from. We lost an afternoon of merchandising work and a customer demo slipped a week.

We have just connected Devin to the repository. Before Monday's new starter arrives, I want Devin to be the thing that makes Harbourline safe and fast to set up — for Devin itself and for a human. Concretely: the machine Devin works on should come up ready to run every gate without anyone typing setup commands; the instructions Devin reads should be the right ones, in the right place, and short enough that it actually reads them; and nobody — human or Devin — should be able to reset a database that is not their own lab database by following our docs.

I am not an engineer by background any more. When you report back, explain what you changed in words I can repeat to our hiring manager, and tell me what you would not hand to Devin.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • In the room: me (Head of Engineering); Priya Nair, the hiring manager who owns the onboarding checklist; Jonas Weber, Trailhead's tech lead, who still has a personal setup note he trusts more than the repo; the new starter, who joins Monday and will be watching.
  • Forbidden: no change to any planted defect (packages/contracts/seed-spec.yaml#planted_edge_cases) and no change to frozen files (packages/contracts/*, .devin/skills/articulation-base/). Proposals for shared files go in your PR description, not in the file.
  • Never run db:seed -- --reset, a migration or docker compose down -v against anything except the local lab database; never write a secret value into a blueprint, an AGENTS.md or a skill — names of variables only.
  • One source of truth: when two documents disagree you must say which one wins and why, and remove or correct the loser on a throwaway branch — not by adding a third document.
  • Deadline: Friday 17:00, so Monday's starter uses the result. A 3-minute update to Priya at the end.
  • Budget: one Devin session for the work; you decide where Ask Devin is enough and where you need an Agent session.

4.Starting state and reset

Run before you start, and again whenever you want the starting state back:

bash labs/01-onboarding/injectors/inject.sh --revert
pnpm lab:reset -- 01
node labs/01-onboarding/tools/agents-budget.mjs

In a Devin session with this repo selected, type @skills:lab-start 01 to run these steps for you, and @skills:lab-cleanup 01 when you finish. From a terminal: pnpm lab:start -- 01 and pnpm lab:cleanup -- 01.

What “clean” looks like

The injector revert prints reverted and git status --short is clean (no docs/SETUP.md, no services/api/src/AGENTS.md, no labs/01-onboarding/.env.lab). lab:reset reseeds whatever DATABASE_URL names from the seed spec — the local Compose database when it is unset; it has no host guard, so check echo $DATABASE_URL is empty or points at localhost before running it. The budget tool lists six AGENTS.md files, all under the limit, with exit code 0. Without Docker, skip the second command: everything else in this lab needs only the clone.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when someone who was not in the session can see all of the following. Every item can be met with zero credentials beyond the repository connection.

  1. Environment inventory. A short table (in your session notes or labs/01-onboarding/ONBOARDING-NOTES.md, not committed) listing, for each setup step a new engineer needs — runtime, dependency install, Python worker tooling, Postgres, seed, gates — where it is configured today (blueprint section, AGENTS.md, skill, README, .env.example) and whether it is correct. At least one step that is wrong today is identified with the file and line or the blueprint line that proves it.
  2. Blueprint that builds. The repository blueprint in Devin's settings references only paths that exist, installs what the gates need, keeps commands idempotent and holds no secret values. Evidence: the blueprint text before and after, and a session (or snapshot build) where the gates ran without manual setup — or, if you cannot trigger a build, the commands executed by hand on a session VM with their exit codes.
  3. Instruction hierarchy mapped. A list of every AGENTS.md, rule and repo skill Devin will read in this repo, with its size in bytes, what it is for, and what happens to content past the automatic-inclusion limit. The output of node labs/01-onboarding/tools/agents-budget.mjs (before and after any change) is acceptable evidence.
  4. Contradiction resolved. After the contradictory-agents injector, you state which instruction Devin should follow, why (scope, recency, owner), and show the corrected text on a throwaway branch. The contradiction must be gone afterwards, not "explained" by a new file.
  5. Stale setup doc handled. After the stale-setup-doc injector, the stale page is either deleted or reduced to a pointer, on a throwaway branch, and every command it gave that still matters now lives in exactly one place.
  6. Unsafe reset closed. You show that the repo's own instructions now point only at the local lab database: which documented command is the supported reset, which database it touches (DATABASE_URL, and its default), what it does not guard against, and what a new engineer would see if they tried the old note. Quote the command output.
  7. Missing secret behaviour explained. After the missing-paygate-secret injector you explain what happens to payment webhooks when the signing key is absent or different between services, where the key's name is documented for a new engineer, and why its value is not in the blueprint.
  8. Ask vs Agent decision. For at least three questions you had (for example "where is the seed defined?", "why does the worker fail to start?"), say whether you used Ask Devin or an Agent session and one reason each.
  9. Reusable know-how. At least one piece of repeated setup guidance lives as a skill (repo SKILL.md or an organization skill), not as a long paragraph in AGENTS.md, and you can say where your organization's old Knowledge notes now live.
  10. No plant fixed, nothing merged. git log origin/main shows no commit from this lab; your throwaway branch is closed or deleted; pnpm lab:reset -- 01 returns the tree to the starting state.
  11. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) or against a timer, with the gap report or your self-score saved as labs/01-onboarding/ONBOARDING-NOTES.coach.md (not committed).

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This lab uses the standard four-part articulation drill. The coach plays Priya Nair, Harbourline's hiring manager: friendly, not technical, and responsible for Monday's new starter. Everything you say must survive being repeated by someone who has never opened a terminal.

60-second framing for a non-engineer

Say what the problem costs and what will be different on Monday before you say anything about tools. Stems you may use:

  • "Today a new engineer spends … before they can … It costs us …"
  • "The risk I care most about is …"
  • "I am using Devin to … and I am deliberately not using it to …"
  • "You will know it worked when …"

Sinhala-first note: lead with the outcome. Do not walk through the setup steps in order ("first Node, then pnpm, then Docker…") before saying what changes for the new starter.

Think-aloud checkpoints (T+10 and T+40)

The coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next. Use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. At T+10 you are usually still auditing; at T+40 you are usually in the middle of an injector. Name the file you are reading. If you need a moment, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent.

3-minute update to the hiring manager

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. One number per sentence. For every change, say where it lives (machine setup, instructions Devin reads, reusable know-how) in plain words, then the proof. End with one concrete ask of Priya.

Objection round

The coach raises two objections, 90 seconds each: one technical, one about people and adoption. Acknowledge, answer with evidence from your session, and close with a next step. Avoid "basically" and "actually"; do not end on "…right?".

Scores and timings are transcript-based, approximate.
