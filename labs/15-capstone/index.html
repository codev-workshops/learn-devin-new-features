2.Customer brief

From: Amara Okafor, COO, Harbourline To: The Devin lead on duty today Subject: One morning, three problems, no script

Every week someone at Harbourline brings three things to the same stand-up: something broke last night, a brand team wants something new by a date, and legal, finance or security adds a rule we did not have last week. Today that someone is you, and I want to see how you run it with Devin.

You will get one sealed envelope. Inside is a real-shaped morning for one of our brands: an incident that is costing us orders or money right now, a feature a brand lead has promised to a customer, and a constraint you must not break while you do either. Nobody will tell you which Devin capabilities to use. Choosing them, and saying why, is the job.

By the end of the half day I want the incident understood and contained, the feature started the right way, the constraint respected and provable, and an update I could forward to the board without editing. Things will change while you work. That is also the job.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Amara Okafor (COO) — owns the outcome and the budget for the day; she will ask what it cost.
  • The customer in the envelope — a brand lead, a finance owner or a risk owner; the coach plays them on the call.
  • Your facilitator — hands out the envelope when there is no coach call, and answers only "is this in scope?" questions.
  • Constraints
    • The envelope is read to you once. Do not open labs/15-capstone/scenarios/; a learner who has read a scenario file draws a different one.
    • No scaffold: this page offers no stems, no feature list and no plan. The hidden answer is for the debrief, after the coach report.
    • Work on your own leaf branch. Planted defects stay on main; anything you fix is a pull request you close without merging.
    • Never ask anyone for credentials. Where a live integration is missing, use the repo's fixtures and say so.
    • Spend like it is your own money: the COO will ask for the session count and ACU you used.
    • Coaching is started by you, manually, at T+0.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when someone who was not in the room can check every item below from the artefacts alone. The envelope decides the content; the evidence shape is the same for every envelope.

  1. Capstone report. labs/15-capstone/CAPSTONE-REPORT.md exists on your leaf branch (not on main) and opens with three one-sentence problem statements — incident, feature, constraint — in the customer's words.
  2. Capability choices. For each of the three, the report names the Devin capability you used, one sentence on why it beat the alternative you rejected, and a link to the session, scan, review, run or report it produced.
  3. Incident evidence. The incident is reproduced on the local stack (the injector's SYMPTOM: line or an equivalent observation), its cause is located to a file and line, and the containment or fix is a pull request against main that you leave open, with the re-run that proves it.
  4. Feature evidence. The feature is a separate pull request (or a written design if the change at T+60 moved it), sized for review, with tests that fail before and pass after.
  5. Constraint evidence. The report shows, with one artefact per claim, that the constraint held for every session you started — not only the last one.
  6. Change handling. The report has a dated entry for the T+60 change: what you kept, what you dropped, what you re-planned and why.
  7. Blocker handling. The report names the blocker you hit, how you noticed it and what you did instead, without widening a permission or weakening the constraint.
  8. Cost. Sessions started, ACU used per session and in total, and the one step you would not run with Devin next time.
  9. Live versus fixture. A table of every integration you touched and whether it ran live, ran on a fixture, or was not exercised and why.
  10. Coach run. The capstone coach's gap report for your run is on your coaching branch.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

The capstone drill has no scaffold. Nothing on this page tells you what to say; the envelope, the clock and the customer do.

Start the coach yourself at T+0: in the Devin web app, select this repo in Agent mode, type @skills:coach-15-capstone <your-handle> and press the voice-call button. A facilitator running a cohort may add --scenario <id> to that line; otherwise the coach picks an envelope you have not drawn before. Keep the call connected for the whole half day; mute while you work (space to talk).

90-second framing

Right after the envelope. The customer stops talking and the clock starts.

Think-aloud checkpoints (T+30, T+90)

Forty-five seconds each, whenever the coach calls in. The T+90 checkpoint comes after the customer has changed something.

Four-minute customer update

Unscripted. No skeleton is printed here.

Objection round

Three objections, ninety seconds each, each with one follow-up if your answer skips evidence.

About the feedback

The coach grades from the speech transcript in the session history, not from audio. Every clarity and pronunciation line in its report is transcript-based, approximate; timings are ±5 s.

Start the Devin voice coach

In the Devin web app, select this repo in Agent mode, type exactly the line below, then click Start voice call:

@skills:coach-15-capstone <your-handle>

Replace with your handle. If the call connects before the skill activates, press Enter first and then start the call in the same session.

Mute hint: between checkpoints you may mute and keep working; the call stays connected. Hold Space to talk if your client offers push-to-talk, and say “done” when you finish a timed segment.

Caveat: clarity and pronunciation feedback from the coach is transcript-based, approximate. The coach works from the speech transcript in the session history, not from audio, so it cannot judge intonation, stress or vowel quality. Record yourself and listen back for real pronunciation work.
