2.Customer brief

It is 02:55 UTC and I am the incident commander for Harbourline tonight. PagerDuty woke our platform on-call at 02:10 with "negative on-hand for SKU LH-LAMP-03" — the Ember Table Lamp, the hero product of Lumen Home's Lamp Week flash sale. We had three units in the US-NJ warehouse. By the time I was escalated, 40 customers had ordered and paid for a lamp we do not have: roughly $2,360 of orders we cannot fulfil, before vouchers and care-team time. The webhook failure-rate monitor started warning at the same time.

The Lamp Week release (harbourline-api 2026.10.21-lampweek) went out at 22:40 last night. Our on-call followed the runbook bookmarked on his phone, restarted the worker, corrected the stock count by hand and went back to bed. Twenty minutes later it paged again. This is our second overnight page in two weeks, and leadership is already asking whether flash sales should pause.

We have just turned on Devin Oncall. I want it working this incident with you, not instead of you. By 09:00 I need: a clear statement of who is affected and how much it costs; the evidence that connects the page to a cause — monitor, incident, traces, code; the likely cause in words my merchandising lead understands; a mitigation that holds until the morning team is in; and a written incident report I can forward without editing. Tell me also how you set Devin up for this — how much of it you would run in the cheaper mode and why.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • In the room (virtually): me (incident commander, Platform Ops); Sam Ortiz, the platform on-call who took the first page; Maya Perera, Lumen Home merchandising lead (joins at 08:00); the US-NJ warehouse shift lead; the PayGate integration owner.
  • Forbidden tonight: no deploy before 08:00 without my approval; no manual stock edits — that is what made the second page worse; nothing that touches production data; no fix to the code on main (proposals go in the report; demos on throwaway branches you close).
  • Site stays up: Lamp Week continues unless I decide otherwise; your mitigation must keep the storefront serving.
  • Evidence standard: every claim in the report points at something I can open — a monitor id, an incident log entry, a trace id, a file and line, a command output.
  • Deadlines: a first update at T+15 into the incident channel (or labs/12-oncall/INCIDENT-UPDATES.md if you have no channel), the incident report at labs/12-oncall/INCIDENT-REPORT.md by 09:00, post-incident review within three days. Do not commit either file.
  • Budget: you choose the session mode for each part of the work; be ready to defend the cost.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not on the call. Every item can be met on the fixture path with zero credentials.

  1. A responder exists. A Devin Oncall responder of the Alerts type is configured for Harbourline (screenshot of the settings page), with its trigger (PagerDuty on the live path; manual on the fixture path), the repo, and a runbook attached. The page or a manual trigger started at least one investigation session (session link or screenshot).
  2. Impact statement. labs/12-oncall/INCIDENT-REPORT.md opens with one paragraph naming the SKU, the number of affected orders and customers, the money at risk with its currency, and the window (first order beyond stock → page → re-page). Each number cites its source (a shim tool call, a metric point, a log entry).
  3. Evidence chain. The report links, in order, the Datadog monitor (id and threshold), the PagerDuty incident (number, trigger, acknowledgement, the note at 02:20 and the re-trigger), at least one trace (trace id and the span attribute that matters), and the code path in services/api (file and function). A reader can follow the chain without asking you.
  4. Likely cause, with its amplifier. The report states the primary cause and the second defect that made the numbers worse, each with one piece of evidence that would look different if you were wrong. "A race condition" on its own does not count.
  5. Local reproduction. On a local stack, one injector run reproduces the primary cause and its RESULT: line is pasted into the report (on hand before and after, succeeded vs rejected). If you cannot run Docker, say so and cite the trace fixture instead.
  6. Mitigation that respects the constraints. The report lists the mitigation until 08:00 — no deploy, no manual stock edit, site stays up — and how you would know it is holding (which series or monitor, what value). Code fixes appear only as follow-ups with an owner.
  7. Runbook judgement. The report says which runbook you followed after the T+15 injector, which one you rejected, and the observation that decided it.
  8. Alert storm absorbed. After the T+25 injector the report says whether the new alerts are one incident or several, and why.
  9. Mode choice justified. One paragraph says which parts of the investigation ran (or would run) in Lite and which in a full mode, with one reason each and a rough cost comparison.
  10. Updates sent. The T+15 update and the closing update exist (incidents channel thread or labs/12-oncall/INCIDENT-UPDATES.md), each following Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask.
  11. Credential honesty. The report has a short "not exercised" list: which integrations were live, which were fixtures.
  12. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) or against a timer, and the gap report (or your self-score) saved as labs/12-oncall/INCIDENT-REPORT.coach.md. Do not commit it.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

This lab uses the incident version of the four-part articulation drill. The coach plays the Harbourline incident commander: tired, precise, short on patience for jargon. Incident voice means short sentences, the number first, and no speculation without a label ("likely", "confirmed").

45-second incident framing

Shorter than usual — an incident commander gives you less than a minute. Stems you may use:

  • "Right now, … customers are affected by … It is costing …"
  • "What I know for sure is… What I do not know yet is…"
  • "I am using Devin to… because…"
  • "I will call it mitigated when…"

Sinhala-first note: say the impact first. Do not build up through the timeline ("first the deploy, then the newsletter, then…") before saying what is broken.

T+15 incident update (2 minutes)

The coach asks for the first update as if you were posting it to the incident channel. Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Use "confirmed" and "suspected" explicitly. One number per sentence. If a runbook changed your plan, say which one and why in one sentence.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+60)

When the coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next, use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. If you need to read a log, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent. End on a full stop, not on "…no?".

Closing update (3 minutes)

The handover to the morning team. Same skeleton, plus the decision you need from the incident commander. Breathe after each heading word. Drop fillers ("actually", "basically", "so, yeah").

Objection round

The coach raises one commercial and one technical objection, in character, 90 seconds each. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
