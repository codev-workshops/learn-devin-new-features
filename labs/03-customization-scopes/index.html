2.Customer brief

Hi — I'm Dana Okafor, platform engineering lead at Harbourline. Both brands run on one checkout service, and every team now reviews checkout changes with Devin. The trouble is that every team taught Devin its own way: one checklist in a PR template, another in somebody's notes, a third pasted into chat. Last month a review that followed the Lumen Home habit asked a Trailhead change to show tax "as its own line, added on top". It reached staging before QA noticed that every Trailhead basket was 19% more expensive than the price on the product page. Two engineers spent two days unpicking it.

I want one checkout-review practice for both brands, kept in the repository, that I can review in a pull request and that Devin picks up without anyone pasting it in.

I'm Jonas Becker, Trailhead Supply's engineering lead. I am fine with Dana's checklist on one condition: our VAT rules win wherever they apply, and my team can change them without opening a ticket with Platform. Trailhead prices include 19% VAT, shipping is free from €80 gross, and our totals must match what the fulfilment worker computes to the cent. Last quarter we filed six requests to edit a shared instruction file; I do not want a seventh.

Between us: show which instructions Devin will have in front of it when it touches services/api/src/money.ts, prove the two layers do not contradict each other, and tell us who is allowed to change which layer. Personal habits are fine, as long as they stay personal.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • In the room: Dana (Platform lead, owns the shared checklist), Jonas (Trailhead lead, owns the VAT rules) and a reviewer from each team who will use the checklist daily.
  • Forbidden: no change to main — everything happens on a throwaway branch; no change to application code under services/ or apps/; do not fix any planted defect (the rounding drift in money.ts stays); no edits to the existing .devin/rules/repo-conventions.md, .devin/rules/money-and-brands.md or root AGENTS.md.
  • Your own machine: never write to your real home directory for this lab; the global layer is simulated under /tmp/harbourline-home.
  • Shared settings: do not change organization or enterprise settings; describe what would live there instead.
  • Ownership: Trailhead must be able to change its rules without a Platform approver, and Platform must be able to change the shared checklist without a Trailhead approver.
  • Decision deadline: both leads sign off at the end of this session.
  • Budget: one Devin session plus your own verification; no paid integrations.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when someone who was not in the room can see each of the following on your throwaway branch, without re-running anything.

  1. One shared checklist. A Platform-owned rule under .devin/rules/ holds the checkout-review checklist with a glob trigger covering the checkout code of both brands, and a repo skill under .devin/skills/ holds the review procedure. The resolver output for services/api/src/money.ts lists both.
  2. Brand rule in its own file. A Trailhead-owned rule file under .devin/rules/ states the VAT presentation, rounding and free-shipping threshold. The resolver loads it for money.ts and skips it for services/api/src/routes/catalog.ts, with the reason it was skipped.
  3. No contradiction left to chance. The resolver's conflicts list for money.ts contains no unresolved entry; for every shared topic, the evidence names which file owns it and which file explicitly yields.
  4. Ownership is reviewable. .github/CODEOWNERS on your branch sends the shared checklist and skill to Platform and the brand rule files to Trailhead, and your scope matrix (a table in your evidence) lists every layer — enterprise, organization, personal, repo root, nested directory, local file, global — with what lives there and who can change it.
  5. Nested conflict handled. After the nested-conflict injector, you show the resolver output with the unresolved conflict, then the output after your fix, and one sentence on why you kept the deeper file instead of deleting it.
  6. Local override contained. After the local-override injector, git status --short does not list AGENTS.local.md, git check-ignore -v AGENTS.local.md names the .gitignore line that hides it, and you state in one sentence who is affected by it and who is not.
  7. Dead rule found. After the wrong-glob injector, you show the resolver skipping the Trailhead rule for money.ts, the corrected globs, and the resolver loading it again.
  8. Skill names unambiguous. After the global-skill injector, the resolver reports the name collision; after your fix it reports none, and you state which copy you changed and why.
  9. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8), or against a timer if you work offline. Save the gap report next to your evidence; do not commit it.
  10. Clean exit. main is untouched, the throwaway branch is not merged, /tmp/harbourline-home is deleted and the reset commands in section 4 return the repository to the clean state.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

The coach plays both team leads from the brief: Dana (Platform) and Jonas (Trailhead). It asks one question at a time, names who is speaking, times every answer with the shell, stays in character and keeps all feedback for the written report at the end.

60-second framing

Headline first, then one reason, then the proof. Stems you may use:

  • "You will get one checklist, and Trailhead keeps… because…"
  • "Each rule lives in… so that…"
  • "You will see it working when the resolver shows…"

Sinhala-first note: resist building up to the point ("because the staging incident, because two teams, therefore…"). Say the layout first, then why.

Think-aloud checkpoints (T+10 and T+45)

At T+10 and again at T+45 the coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next. Answer with the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. Read a file path aloud slowly, segment by segment ("services, slash, api…"). If you need to read resolver output, say "Give me ten seconds to read this" instead of going silent.

Three-minute update to both team leads

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Address each lead by name at least once and give each one the sentence that answers their own condition. One number or file per sentence. Either lead may interrupt twice in total with a short question; answer it in one sentence and return to the skeleton.

Objection round

Two objections in character, 90 seconds each, one technical and one commercial; the coach may ask one follow-up if your answer skips the evidence. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here — that is the point.
