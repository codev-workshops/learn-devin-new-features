2.Customer brief

Hi — I run customer support at Harbourline, for both Lumen Home (USD) and Trailhead Supply (EUR). Since Tuesday my team has pasted the same complaint into #harbourline-bugs again and again: "customer says she was charged twice and got two order-confirmed emails." Each agent posts it fresh because nobody can tell whether engineering has seen it. Engineering sees a wall of identical messages, tags the wrong team, and the real answer ends up in a DM that support never reads.

What I need from you:

  1. One answer per problem. However many times my team reports the same thing, engineering looks at it once, and every later report points at that first conversation.
  2. The answer where we asked. The diagnosis goes back into the thread where support raised it, in words my team can repeat to a customer, with the team that owns the fix tagged.
  3. Fix the routing once. When an engineer says "not ours", that should stick. I do not want to see the same wrong team tagged tomorrow.
  4. Quiet by default. Lunch, thank-yous and chatter in the channel get nothing back. If my team stops reading the channel because a bot talks too much, we are worse off than today.
  5. Safe with what we paste. My team pastes customer tickets verbatim. Some of them are rude, some are strange. Nothing pasted into the channel should be able to make the bot reach out to the internet or leak anything.

We also have a Microsoft Teams workspace for the warehouse team; tell me whether the same setup would work there. You have ninety minutes. At the end I want a short Slack update I can forward to my managers.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Me (support lead) owns the channel and decides what my team will read. I am not an engineer; I read Slack, not code.
  • Engineering owners (@harbourline-api, @harbourline-fulfilment, @harbourline-web) fix what is routed to them. One of them will correct a routing mistake in a thread.
  • The platform lead owns main. The planted defects stay in the baseline; any demonstration change goes on a throwaway lab08/* branch and is deleted without merging.
  • Forbidden: posting into any shared Slack or Teams channel (use a test channel only you are in, a DM to yourself, or the offline export), real customer data, credentials in any file, changes on main, and changes to packages/contracts/.
  • Output: the responder's setup text (kept out of commits), your scratchpad export, the thread write-backs you would post, and the ≤120-word Slack update. Keep notes out of commits.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when someone who was not in the room can see each of the following.

  1. Right tool, stated. Your notes say which product surface watches the channel (an Oncall Support + Bugs responder, or the Triage Bug Reports automation template) and why, including what the documentation says about the older one.
  2. Noise stays quiet. With the noise injector, the replay (node labs/08-slack-triage/solution/triage-sim.mjs --inject noise) lists the three chatty messages as ignored, and your setup text says what the responder must ignore.
  3. Duplicates grouped. With every injector applied, the channel holds exactly four issues. Every repeat of the double-charge complaint, including the paraphrased ones, is linked to the first report's thread, and you predicted the count before running the replay.
  4. Scratchpad checked. Your scratchpad export passes triage-sim.mjs --inject all --check <file> with no problems: each triaged report has channel, timestamp and reporter; duplicates are linked; the routing table is current.
  5. Routing corrected once. After the engineer's reply in the first thread, the routing table names the new owner, the replay shows one re-route reply in that same thread, and later matching reports go to the new owner without another correction.
  6. Answers in the thread. You have one write-back draft per issue, posted to the first report's thread, that names the cause in plain words, points to the file, tags the owner and says who confirms before anyone acts. Repeats get a reaction, not a new message.
  7. Fewer messages than reports. Your notes give the replay's post count against its report count, and you can say what a reader in the channel sees for each kind of message.
  8. Private channel handled honestly. For the forward from a private channel, your write-back asks for the details or for Devin to be added, and does not guess at the content.
  9. Untrusted input contained. For the pasted ticket, the replay shows the requested host blocked and the report still grouped; your setup turns on the responder's network policy with an allowlist, and your notes say why messages in this channel count as untrusted.
  10. Teams variant answered. Your notes say how the same setup runs on a Microsoft Teams channel (--platform teams), and one thing that is different there.
  11. Slack update written. A Slack message of at most 120 words to the support lead: what changed, what her team will see, what you still need. Then the articulation drill with the voice coach (section 8), or against a timer offline. The gap report (or self-score) is saved next to your notes and not committed.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

The coach plays Harbourline's head of customer support. She reads Slack all day, not code, and judges every change by what her team will see in the channel.

60-second framing

Lead with what her team stops doing, then what they will see instead, then what you need from her. Stems you can use:

  • "Your team will stop re-posting because…"
  • "When someone reports a problem, they will see…"
  • "What I need from you is…"

Sinhala-first note: say the outcome for the support team first, before naming responders, triggers or files.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+10)

The coach asks what you are looking at and what you will check next. Answer with I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. At T+10 you should be deciding what the triage remembers between messages and how a repeat is recognised, not polishing the wording of a diagnosis.

Think-aloud checkpoint (T+45)

Same four verbs, later in the block. By now a pasted ticket or a forwarded message has surprised you. Name the surprise and the setting or replay output you checked. If you need to read the replay, say "Give me ten seconds to read the output" rather than going silent.

Slack update to the support lead

Write it first: at most 120 words, as a Slack message she could forward. Skeleton: What changed → What your team will see → What is still open → Ask. Then read it aloud in 90 seconds. Use counts from the replay, not adjectives. Pause after each heading word.

Objection round

She raises one technical and one commercial objection, in character, about 90 seconds each. Reply with: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The commercial objection needs a number from the replay about messages or posts. The objections themselves are not printed here.
