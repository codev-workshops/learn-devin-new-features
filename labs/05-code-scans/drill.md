The coach plays Dana, the Harbourline CTO, with the security lead joining for the objection round. It follows the base articulation protocol: framing, two think-aloud checkpoints, a three-minute update, then three objections.

### 60-second framing

Headline first: what is at risk, which kinds of scan you will run and why, and what the CTO will have at the end. Stems you may use:

- "The risk is… for both brands…"
- "I will run… because… and not… because…"
- "You will get… by…"

Sinhala-first note: name the decision before the reasons. "Three scans, here is why" — then the why.

### Think-aloud checkpoints (T+20 and T+90)

At T+20 you are usually reading the Findings tab; at T+90 you are usually deciding what to assign and what to throw out. Use the four verbs: *I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…*. Read a finding's title aloud before you judge it. Say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent.

### Three-minute update to the CTO

Skeleton: **Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask.** Every sentence carries one number: findings by severity, statements per page load, minutes to a proposed fix, findings you threw out. Name the files a reviewer would open. Close with one concrete ask.

### Objection round

Three objections — technical, commercial, governance — 90 seconds each, in character. Reply pattern: **acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer**. The objections are not printed here; that is the point.
