2.Customer brief

Hi — this is Dana, CTO of Harbourline. Two things landed on my desk on the same morning.

First, a Lumen Home merchandiser opened an order link a colleague pasted in chat and saw a Trailhead Supply customer's order — lines, totals, shipping country. Same database, same API, different brand. I need to know whether that was a one-off or whether any merchandiser can read any brand's orders, and whether there are more holes of the same family. Our payments partner also asked last quarter whether we verify their webhook signatures; I said yes, and I would like that to be true.

Second, the Lamp Week flash-sale collection page on Lumen is slow. It is our biggest week; we expect several times our normal ~400 orders a week. The page was fine with ten products and is not fine now.

We just switched on Devin Code Scans. I want you to use it properly — not as "run it and paste the list" but as a lifecycle: pick the right kinds of scan, read the findings like an engineer, get one real fix proposed by Devin, throw out what is noise with a reason, show me the scan staying current after a change, and give our security team an export they can load into their own tooling. Then explain it to me in three minutes with numbers.

3.Stakeholders and constraints

  • Dana (CTO) — sponsor; wants numbers and a yes/no on "are we leaking across brands".
  • Security lead — owns the existing SAST pipeline and the dashboard it feeds; sceptical of a second tool.
  • Platform team — will triage whatever you leave open; their time is the scarce resource.
  • Merchandisers (both brands) — use the /admin console daily, one of them with a screen reader.
  • Forbidden: merging any fix into main. Harbourline's known defects stay in place for the course; any fix Devin proposes goes to a throwaway branch or a PR you close unmerged.
  • Forbidden: creating Automations or schedules for scans in this lab; every scan run is started by you.
  • Budget: at most three scans plus one Scan-new-commits run; Normal effort unless you can justify Deep.
  • Deliverable: evidence for each acceptance criterion (finding links or screenshots, the PR link, the export file) collected in a local labs/05-code-scans/EVIDENCE.md that you do not commit.

5.Acceptance criteria

You are done when all of the following are observable by someone who was not in the room. Collect the evidence in labs/05-code-scans/EVIDENCE.md (do not commit it).

  1. Brand-isolation finding. A Security scan on main has an open finding whose location is GET /orders/:orderId in services/api/src/routes/orders.ts, and you can explain in one sentence why a Lumen merchandiser can read a Trailhead order (the lookup is not constrained by the caller's X-Harbourline-Brand).
  2. Webhook-signature finding. The same scan, or a second Security run, has an open finding on POST /webhooks/payments in services/api/src/routes/webhooks.ts: the HMAC is computed but never compared with X-PayGate-Signature.
  3. Performance finding. A Performance scan has a finding on the collection listing (GET /catalog/collections/:slug in services/api/src/routes/catalog.ts → buildProduct in services/api/src/services/catalog.ts) that states the per-product query pattern, and your evidence gives the statement count for the Lamp Week collection (from the finding or from the lamp-week-proof injector).
  4. Accessibility finding in admin. An Accessibility scan has at least one finding under apps/web/src/admin/ (unnamed icon buttons on the inventory page, or the low-contrast price badge), with the WCAG success criterion it violates.
  5. Assigned to Devin, PR produced. Exactly one finding is assigned to Devin from the Findings tab; the resulting remediation session opens a pull request; the PR link is in your evidence and the PR is closed without merging (or targets a throwaway branch). main still contains the plant.
  6. Dismissed with a note. Exactly one finding is dismissed with a written reason a security reviewer would accept (for example a fixture-only secret, or a duplicate of another finding), and the note is visible on the finding.
  7. Scan new commits after the PR. After the PR step you ran Scan new commits on the same scan and recorded what it reported, including why nothing new appears when no commit has landed on main.
  8. SARIF export. A SARIF 2.1.0 file exported from the scan is saved locally; you can point at one result in it and match its ruleId, location and level to the Findings tab.
  9. Profile choice is justified. Your evidence names the scan type and profile (or defaults plus guidance) used for each scan, the include/exclude globs, the effort level, and the permission (Use vs Manage) each step needed.
  10. Articulation drill completed with the voice coach (section 8) and the gap report saved as labs/05-code-scans/EVIDENCE.report.md. Do not commit it.

8.Articulation drill (voice coach)

The coach plays Dana, the Harbourline CTO, with the security lead joining for the objection round. It follows the base articulation protocol: framing, two think-aloud checkpoints, a three-minute update, then three objections.

60-second framing

Headline first: what is at risk, which kinds of scan you will run and why, and what the CTO will have at the end. Stems you may use:

  • "The risk is… for both brands…"
  • "I will run… because… and not… because…"
  • "You will get… by…"

Sinhala-first note: name the decision before the reasons. "Three scans, here is why" — then the why.

Think-aloud checkpoints (T+20 and T+90)

At T+20 you are usually reading the Findings tab; at T+90 you are usually deciding what to assign and what to throw out. Use the four verbs: I see… I think… I'll try… I'll check…. Read a finding's title aloud before you judge it. Say "Give me ten seconds to read this" rather than going silent.

Three-minute update to the CTO

Skeleton: Outcome → Evidence → Risk → Next action → Ask. Every sentence carries one number: findings by severity, statements per page load, minutes to a proposed fix, findings you threw out. Name the files a reviewer would open. Close with one concrete ask.

Objection round

Three objections — technical, commercial, governance — 90 seconds each, in character. Reply pattern: acknowledge → boundary → evidence → offer. The objections are not printed here; that is the point.
